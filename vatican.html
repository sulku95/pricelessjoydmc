Vatican City

Vatican City, the world's smallest sovereign state nestled within the heart of Rome, is one of the most extraordinary destinations on earth, drawing millions of pilgrims and travellers alike with its unparalleled concentration of sacred art, history, and spiritual significance. Home to the Pope, the breathtaking St. Peter's Basilica, and the legendary Sistine Chapel, this tiny enclave holds treasures that have shaped Western civilisation for over two millennia. A visit here is nothing short of transformative — an immersive journey through faith, power, and the very pinnacle of human artistic achievement.

Must-See Experiences

Top Highlights

01

Sistine Chapel Ceiling

Marvel at Michelangelo's breathtaking frescoes, including the iconic Creation of Adam, adorning the ceiling and altar wall of this sacred papal chapel.

02

St. Peter's Basilica Dome

Climb or take the elevator to the summit of St. Peter's Basilica's magnificent dome for panoramic views across Rome and the Vatican Gardens.

03

Vatican Museums Collection

Wander through centuries of papal collections spanning ancient sculpture, Renaissance masterpieces, and Egyptian antiquities across 54 galleries.

04

St. Peter's Square

Stand in the vast elliptical square embraced by Bernini's sweeping colonnade, the grand gateway to the heart of Catholic Christendom.

05

Castel Sant'Angelo

Explore the imposing circular mausoleum-turned-fortress that once sheltered popes during times of crisis, now offering spectacular views over the Tiber.

06

Vatican Gardens Tour

Stroll through the meticulously manicured Vatican Gardens, a serene green oasis covering half the city-state and reserved for the Pope's private retreat.

Did You Know?

Fun Facts

Vatican City is the world's smallest independent state, covering just 44 hectares — smaller than many city parks.

The Vatican has its own telephone system, post office, radio station, and even issues its own euro coins, which are highly sought after by collectors.

Michelangelo painted the entire Sistine Chapel ceiling largely alone, lying on scaffolding for four years between 1508 and 1512.

The Swiss Guard, who protect the Pope, must be Catholic Swiss males and still wear a colourful Renaissance-era uniform said to have been designed by Michelangelo himself.
