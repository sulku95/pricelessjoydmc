Spain

Spain is a country of extraordinary contrasts, where sun-drenched Mediterranean coastlines meet snow-capped mountain peaks, and centuries of Moorish, Roman, and Christian heritage blend seamlessly with a cutting-edge culinary and design scene. From the architectural wonders of Barcelona and the royal grandeur of Madrid to the soulful flamenco rhythms of Seville and the mystical Alhambra of Granada, every region offers a deeply distinct and unforgettable cultural experience. With world-class cuisine, a legendary festival calendar, and some of Europe's most diverse landscapes, Spain captivates every traveller from the very first visit.

Must-See Experiences

Top Highlights

01

Alhambra Palace Granada

A stunning UNESCO-listed hilltop palace complex featuring exquisite Moorish architecture, intricate tilework, and lush Generalife gardens overlooking Granada.

02

Sagrada Família Barcelona

Antoni Gaudí's awe-inspiring unfinished basilica in Barcelona is a masterpiece of organic architecture and one of Europe's most visited landmarks.

03

Prado Museum Madrid

Spain's legendary art museum in Madrid houses a world-class collection including masterworks by Velázquez, Goya, and El Greco spanning centuries of European painting.

04

Seville's Old City

A vibrant Andalusian city celebrated for its passionate flamenco traditions, ornate cathedral housing Columbus's tomb, and the magnificent Real Alcázar palace.

05

Camino de Santiago

The Camino de Santiago is an ancient pilgrimage network of trails crossing Spain, culminating at the magnificent Santiago de Compostela Cathedral in Galicia.

06

Andalusian Cliff Villages

Perched dramatically on clifftops above the Mediterranean, the whitewashed villages of Costa Brava and Ronda offer breathtaking scenery and authentic Andalusian charm.

Did You Know?

Fun Facts

Spain is home to the world's oldest restaurant, Sobrino de Botín in Madrid, which has been serving customers since 1725 and holds a Guinness World Record.

Spain has more UNESCO World Heritage Sites than almost any other country in the world, with over 50 recognized cultural and natural treasures.

The Spanish city of Valencia is the birthplace of paella, and the dish was originally a peasant meal cooked by farmers using whatever ingredients were available in the rice fields.

Spain's La Tomatina festival in Buñol sees tens of thousands of participants hurl over 100 tonnes of ripe tomatoes at each other in the world's largest food fight.
